# Token budget governor (P-058) — 2026-11-07

Slice: **P-058**, Part IV — Prompt & Sampler Depth (P-049 – P-064).

Roadmap line: *Token budget governor: the composer shows the whole prompt's budget (context, lore, memory, history) and trims by declared policy when over.*

## What was built

- `src/runmal/prompt/governor.py` — NEW pure-domain module (no DB, no I/O, no migration; head stays 0034). It answers, before anything is sent, what the WHOLE prompt costs per section, and — when a declared trim policy is in play — applies that policy to the slot list deterministically, recording every sacrifice it makes. The module is the P-044 `lorebooks/accounting.py` shape, one package over: P-044 accounted lore only; the governor accounts all four sections.

### Sections and the kind mapping

- `SECTIONS = ("context", "lore", "memory", "history")` — all four sections are always present in every budget, zero rows included.
- `section_for_kind(kind) -> str` maps a slot kind to exactly one section: `lore_before` / `lore_after` -> `lore`; `memory_nucleus` / `memory_retrieved` -> `memory`; `history` -> `history`; EVERY other kind -> `context`. "Every other kind" is exhaustive by construction: `main_system`, `persona`, `examples`, `authors_note`, `post_history`, and any unknown or future kind all fall to `context`. Because the fallback is total, the four sections always sum to the whole prompt — reconciliation by construction, never by a leftover bucket the caller must remember to add. (The mapping generalises trace.py's `LORE_KINDS` / `MEMORY_KINDS` frozensets to the full kind space.)

### The whole-prompt budget (the show-the-whole-budget half)

- `budget_from_slots(slots, *, input_budget, counter=None) -> WholePromptBudget` counts each slot once, per slot, through the existing `slots.slot_tokens(slot, counter)` seam, and groups the counts by `section_for_kind(slot.kind)`. `input_budget` is validated: it must be an `int` (bools rejected) and `>= 0`, else `ValueError`.
- `WholePromptBudget` (frozen dataclass): `sections` (dict, section name -> `SectionBudget`), `total_tokens` (the sum over sections — the whole prompt), `input_budget` (the budget the total is measured against), `remaining` (`input_budget - total_tokens`, never clamped: a negative remainder is the honest signal of an over-budget list), `over_budget` (`total_tokens > input_budget`), and `count_quality` (`TokenQuality.EXACT` when a counter was supplied, else `ESTIMATED`).
- `SectionBudget` (frozen dataclass): `section`, `tokens` (the section total), `slots` (the NUMBER of slots in the section), `slot_ids` (their ids, in input order). `WholePromptBudget.to_dict()` is the plain JSON-shaped form: `sections` maps each name to `{"tokens": int, "slots": int, "slot_ids": [str, ...]}`, plus `total_tokens`, `input_budget`, `remaining`, `over_budget`, and `count_quality` as its string value. The budget carries aggregates and ids only — no per-slot token breakdown and no content.
- `budget_from_trace(trace) -> dict` is the trace-side twin, exactly the `account_from_trace` pattern (P-044): a pure function over an inspect trace dict (the `trace_to_dict` shape) that counts nothing itself — it sums the composer's own manifest numbers carried in the trace, so the inspector's budget reconciles with the ledger by construction. Per section it reports `included_tokens` and `discarded_tokens` (trace slots grouped by `section_for_kind(kind)`, split on the trace slot's `status`) plus `slots`, the count of trace rows in that section. The whole-prompt totals are read straight from the trace's ledger: `total_input_tokens`, `input_budget`, `remaining_input_tokens`, and `count_quality`. Because every trace slot's kind maps to exactly one section, the per-section included sums add up to the ledger's `total_input_tokens` exactly.

### The trim policy, declared as data

- `BudgetPolicy` (frozen dataclass): `section_caps: dict[str, int | None]` — per-section token caps; a missing section means uncapped, and an explicit `None` also means uncapped — and `trim_order: tuple[str, ...]` — the sections in sacrifice order: which section gives up material first when the total budget bites.
- `DEFAULT_TRIM_ORDER = ("lore", "memory", "history", "context")` — lore is sacrificed first; `context` (the system / persona framing) is sacrificed last. `default_policy() -> BudgetPolicy` is no caps (every section uncapped) plus the default trim order.
- Validation lives in the dataclass itself (`__post_init__` normalises and validates), and `policy_from_data(data) -> BudgetPolicy` / `policy_to_data(policy) -> dict` are the strict boundary with supplied data, in the `authors_note.note_from_data` / `manager.layout_from_data` style: a malformed policy is refused, never repaired silently and never partially applied.
  - Unknown top-level keys raise `ValueError` (only `section_caps` and `trim_order` are allowed); unknown section names (in `section_caps` or `trim_order`) raise `ValueError`.
  - A cap must be an `int >= 1` or `None`; bools are rejected (`isinstance(x, bool)` checked before `int`, the authors_note discipline), as are non-ints and values `< 1`.
  - `trim_order` must name each section at most once; duplicates raise `ValueError`. At the `policy_from_data` boundary `trim_order`, when present and not `None`, must be a list. Sections missing from `trim_order` are APPENDED in default order — the P-049 fill-missing precedent (`layout_from_data` fills unmentioned blocks from the default layout) — so a partial policy still denotes a complete one: after normalisation `trim_order` always names all four sections exactly once.
  - `policy_from_data` fills the caps out in full (every section present, missing -> `None`), and `policy_to_data` serialises the normalised policy in full (every section's cap, the complete trim order), so an echo of the policy is the policy actually applied, never the partial form the caller happened to send. (Called directly with `None`, `policy_from_data` returns `default_policy()`; the service layer below gives `None` a different meaning — no policy in play — see Wiring.)
- Trace discard reasons minted by this slice, exported as `SECTION_CAP_REASON = "governor_section_cap"` (a slot dropped / trimmed because its section exceeded its declared cap) and `TOTAL_BUDGET_REASON = "governor_total_budget"` (a slot dropped / trimmed because the whole prompt exceeded the input budget).

### Trimming: `apply_policy`

- `apply_policy(slots, policy, *, input_budget, counter=None) -> GovernedSlots`. `GovernedSlots` is the frozen pair `(kept, actions)`: the surviving slot list (input order preserved) and the tuple of `GovernorAction` records for everything the governor touched. A `policy` of `None` is treated as `default_policy()` at this level. `input_budget` is validated as above. Every slot is counted once up front through `slot_tokens`; a slot whose parts are trimmed is recounted after each trim. Same slots + same policy + same budget + same counter -> identical kept list and identical actions, always.
- `GovernorAction` (frozen dataclass): `slot_id`, `section`, `tokens_before`, `tokens_after` (0 for a drop), `action` (`"dropped"` | `"trimmed"`), `reason` (`governor_section_cap` | `governor_total_budget`), `trimmed_parts` (count of splittable parts removed; 0 for a plain drop; for a dropped splittable slot, all of its parts). Actions are inspectable data — ids, sections, counts, reasons only, never content (S214).
- Phase 1 — section caps first, in `SECTIONS` order. Within a capped section whose total exceeds its cap, sacrifice one step at a time until the section fits or no sacrificable material remains. The within-section victim order for plain slots is fully deterministic, never encounter-order luck: lowest `priority` first; ties broken by higher `order` first, then `id` descending.
- Splittable slots go first: in either phase, when a section has a splittable candidate with parts remaining (in practice the `history` slot, the composer's splittable contract), that candidate is sacrificed before any plain slot in the section, regardless of priority. Its sacrifice is part-wise, oldest parts first: one part is removed (`parts[1:]`) per step. A part-trim that leaves at least one part yields a `trimmed` action and the surviving slot stays in `kept`, composed later from its trimmed parts; the slot is dropped only when its last part is removed (zero parts survive), and that drop records `trimmed_parts` = all of them.
- Phase 2 — total budget next. While the kept total still exceeds `input_budget`, sweep the policy's `trim_order` in order, taking ONE sacrifice step per section per sweep, and repeat sweeps until the total fits or a full sweep makes no progress.
- Actions are coalesced per slot per phase: repeated steps on the same slot within one phase merge into a single `GovernorAction` whose `tokens_before` is the count before the phase first touched the slot and whose `trimmed_parts` are summed (a drop wins over a trim). A slot touched in both phases therefore carries two actions, one per reason, in phase order. A slot the governor did not touch yields no action.
- Required slots are NEVER dropped or trimmed by the governor, in either phase (see the honesty contract). Depth slots (`Slot.depth is not None`, the S206 splice citizens) get no special treatment and no special protection: the governor has no operation that rewrites a slot's depth or splices content, and a depth slot is an ordinary slot of its section for dropping.
- The package surface is exported from `runmal.prompt`: `SECTIONS`, `SECTION_CAP_REASON`, `TOTAL_BUDGET_REASON`, `DEFAULT_TRIM_ORDER`, `section_for_kind`, `SectionBudget`, `WholePromptBudget`, `budget_from_slots`, `budget_from_trace`, `BudgetPolicy`, `default_policy`, `policy_from_data`, `policy_to_data`, `GovernorAction`, `GovernedSlots`, `apply_policy`.

## Wiring

- `src/runmal/prompt/service.py` gains, mirroring `resolve_layout` / `layout_echo` exactly: `resolve_policy(explicit) -> (BudgetPolicy | None, source)` — an explicit policy is validated by `policy_from_data` (`ValueError` propagates to the caller, which maps it to 422) and returned with source `"explicit"`; `None` yields `(None, "default")` — no policy in play, pipeline unchanged — and `policy_echo(policy, source) -> dict`, the `budget_governor` policy echo: `{"source": source, "policy": policy_to_data(...)}`, where a `None` policy echoes `default_policy()` (the `layout_echo` precedent: the echo shows the default the caller would get, labelled with its true source).
- `src/runmal/prompt/inspect.py`: `inspect_prompt` gains a `budget_policy` parameter, resolved via `resolve_policy` alongside the existing layout / template resolutions. When a policy is supplied it is applied AFTER layout filtering and AFTER context-template filtering, and BEFORE `compose` — the governor governs the exact slot list the composer would otherwise have received. It runs as `apply_policy(slots, policy, input_budget=context_limit - reserve_response)`; the inspector composes with the composer's default overhead of 0, so that number is the same input budget the composer's ledger then reports. Governor-DROPPED slots enter the trace as extra discards (the `build_trace(..., extra_discards=...)` channel the layout / context stages use): `TraceSlot`s with the governor reason and `tokens` = the action's `tokens_before` (the full pre-governor count). A governor-TRIMMED history slot is not a discard: it is composed from its trimmed parts, and its trim is visible in the `budget_governor` actions.
- The result ALWAYS gains `"budget"` = `budget_from_trace(result)` — computed over the finished trace dict, policy or not. This is the show-the-whole-budget half: every inspect result now shows the whole prompt's per-section cost against the ledger. The result also gains `"budget_governor"` = `{"applied": bool, "source": source, "policy": <policy echo>, "actions": [...]}` — `applied` is True exactly when a policy was supplied and `apply_policy` ran; with no policy it is False, `actions` is empty, the source is `"default"`, and the echo shows the default policy.
- `src/runmal/prompt/router.py`: `InspectRequest` gains `budget_policy: dict | None = None`, passed through to `inspect_prompt` on `POST /debug/prompt-inspect`; an invalid policy raises `ValueError` inside the service and surfaces as 422 via the route's existing `ValueError` -> 422 mapping. No new error machinery. New `GET /debug/budget-policy/default` returns the default policy echo (`{"policy": policy_to_data(default_policy()), "source": "default"}`), following the `/debug/prompt-manager/default` precedent.

## Honesty contract

1. One counter, from `runmal.tokens`: every count in the governor comes through `slot_tokens` — `estimate_tokens` by default, or the caller's counter. The heuristic is never re-implemented locally (no `len(text) // 4` in the governor module), so governor counts are the composer's spend for the same slots: composer and governor share the `slot_tokens` seam, and the number that decides a sacrifice is the number the composer would have spent. `budget_from_slots` labels its quality EXACT iff a counter was supplied, else ESTIMATED (the P-044 rule, identical to `PromptManifest.count_quality`); `budget_from_trace` carries the ledger's own `count_quality`.
2. `budget_from_trace` reconciles EXACTLY with the ledger: it sums the composer's own manifest numbers carried in the trace and reads its totals from the ledger itself, and the exhaustive kind mapping means the per-section included sums equal the ledger's `total_input_tokens` — there is no unaccounted remainder.
3. Required slots are never trimmed: they are protected in both phases. If required slots alone exceed the budget there is nothing honest left to sacrifice: the governor returns them untouched and still over budget, and the composer's existing `ImpossiblePromptError` remains the honest failure downstream. The governor never manufactures a fit by eating the framing.
4. Nothing is silent: every drop and every trim yields a `GovernorAction`; the action list is the complete account of the policy's effect on this prompt. Symmetrically, an over-budget result stays visibly over: `remaining` is never clamped, and a kept list that still exceeds the budget is returned as-is, not quietly cut further.
5. No content in payloads (S214): `WholePromptBudget.to_dict()`, `GovernorAction`, the `budget` / `budget_governor` result keys, and the default-policy endpoint carry slot ids, sections, token counts, statuses and reasons only — never slot text.
6. No-policy pipeline unchanged: with no `budget_policy` supplied, the slots reaching the composer, the trace, and every pre-existing result key are exactly as before P-058; the only additions to the result are the two new keys (`budget`, and `budget_governor` with `applied: False`).

## Worked example

All counts use the canonical estimator, `estimate_tokens(text) = max(1, len(text) // 4)`. Six slots go in (history text is its parts joined by `"\n"`, the `slot_text` rule):

| slot | kind -> section | setup | tokens |
|---|---|---|---|
| `sys` | `main_system` -> context | required, 40 chars | 10 |
| `persona` | `persona` -> context | 32 chars | 8 |
| `lore/a` | `lore_before` -> lore | priority 10, 80 chars | 20 |
| `lore/b` | `lore_before` -> lore | priority 50, 40 chars | 10 |
| `mem/1` | `memory_retrieved` -> memory | 40 chars | 10 |
| `history` | `history` -> history | splittable, 3 parts of 40 chars: joined 122 chars | 30 |

`budget_from_slots(slots, input_budget=38)` reports context 18, lore 30, memory 10, history 30 — `total_tokens` 88, `remaining` -50, `over_budget` True, `count_quality` `estimated`.

Declared policy: `{"section_caps": {"lore": 12}}` — normalised to caps `{context: None, lore: 12, memory: None, history: None}` with the default trim order `(lore, memory, history, context)`. `apply_policy(slots, policy, input_budget=38)`:

1. Phase 1 (cap): lore totals 30 > cap 12. The lowest-priority lore slot goes first: `lore/a` is dropped (20 tokens). Lore now totals 10, under the cap. Action: `lore/a`, dropped, `governor_section_cap`, 20 -> 0, `trimmed_parts` 0. Kept total: 68.
2. Phase 2 (total): 68 > 38, so the trim order is swept. Lore first: `lore/b` is dropped (`governor_total_budget`, 10 -> 0); total 58. Memory next: `mem/1` is dropped (`governor_total_budget`, 10 -> 0); total 48. History next: the splittable slot is trimmed part-wise, oldest part first — 2 parts survive (joined 81 chars), so history counts 30 -> 20 (`trimmed`, `governor_total_budget`, `trimmed_parts` 1); total 38. The budget is met mid-sweep, so the sweep stops before `context`: `persona` survives, and required `sys` was never a candidate in any case.

Kept, in input order: `sys` (10), `persona` (8), `history` trimmed to 2 parts (20) — total 38, exactly the input budget. Four actions in total: one under `governor_section_cap`, three under `governor_total_budget`. (This example was executed against the shipped module; the numbers above are its actual output.)

## Deliberate boundaries

- No persistence, no migration: the policy is request data only, resolved per composition — the P-049 layout precedent (explicit > default, no stored tier; a service that pretended to store one would be a lie). Storing a policy per owner or per chat is a named follow-up slice that would own its own migration. The migration head stays 0034.
- No generation-path wiring: the inspector is the visible-before-sending surface, and it is the only consumer in this slice — nothing on the send path calls the governor. The P-059 prompt preview is the natural next consumer of `apply_policy` / `budget_from_slots`.
- The composer's own overflow rules are unchanged when no policy is in play: greedy admission, `over_input_budget` / `over_slot_budget` discards, the per-slot `Slot.slot_budget` cap, and `ImpossiblePromptError` all behave exactly as before. The governor runs before the composer only when a policy is explicitly supplied, and its own caps are per-SECTION and per-TOTAL only — it adds no per-slot cap.
- Required-over-cap stays visibly over rather than being silently cut: when the required slots alone exceed the budget, the governor sacrifices nothing it is forbidden to touch, returns the list still over, and leaves the failure to the composer's `ImpossiblePromptError` — see honesty clauses 3 and 4.
